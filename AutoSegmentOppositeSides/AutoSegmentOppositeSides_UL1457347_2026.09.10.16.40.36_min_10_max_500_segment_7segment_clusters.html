AutoSegment segment clusters with opposite parent sides


The AutoSegment with opposite parent sides groups attempts to cluster overlapping segments into separate groups.


The underlying idea is that overlapping segments of DNA that you share with your DNA matches can be either on one side, or on both sides. In the first scenario, when the segments are on one side, all DNA segments will triangulate with eachother. In the second scenario, when not all segments triangulate with eachother, two groups of segments should appear. One group linked to each parent side.

The table shows how 243 DNA segments (linked to 205 DNA matches) are linked to 52 overlapping segment clusters. These 52 overlapping segment clusters are further partitioned into 175 segment clusters using triangulation data.

Previously, AutoSegment would discard single segments in segment clusters that are not triangulating with the other segments. Now, individual segments are kept and partitioned into different groups, resulting in more segment clusters.

One application of having the opposite side information for segments is the ability to obtain clues if the parents of the tested person are from a different background. Also, if there are no or almost no opposite sided segment clusters, this might be an indication that one of the parents comes from an underrepresented population in the database. In this case, make sure to examine the segments clusters that do show signs of opposite sides, since they may provide clues to the etnicity ofone of the parents.

Last, it is possible to import the (opposite) segment clusters into Cluster Auto Painter on the DNA Painter site.
